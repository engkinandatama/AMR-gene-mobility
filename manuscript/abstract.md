## Structured version (default)

**Background.** The human gut is a major reservoir of antimicrobial resistance
genes (ARGs), but the public health risk posed by that reservoir depends less on
which resistance genes are present than on whether they can move. Mobile genetic
elements — plasmids, integrons and insertion sequences — supply mechanistically
distinct routes by which resistance can reach pathogens. Population-scale
resistome surveys have largely quantified gene content from unassembled reads, an
approach that cannot resolve the genomic context of a gene. Consequently, while
the geography of ARG content is well described, the geography of ARG
mobilization remains largely uncharacterized.

**Methods.** We analysed 248 publicly available gut metagenomes from healthy,
antibiotic-free participants in five countries spanning four geographic regions
(Denmark, China, India, Cameroon and Madagascar). Each metagenome was assembled
independently, and high-confidence ARGs were identified against CARD at ≥95%
identity and ≥95% reference coverage. Plasmids, integrons and insertion sequences
were profiled on the same contigs using three orthogonal tools, and every ARG was
assigned to the mobile element with which it was physically linked. We summarized
per-sample mobilization with a Resistome Mobility Index, tested geographic
differences using a generalized linear mixed model with a per-sample random
effect, assessed decoupling between resistome and taxonomic composition, and
compared ARG–element association networks between populations.
